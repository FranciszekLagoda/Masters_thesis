### 21.11.2026 - Literature review topics
To test the question: *Can a country develop without changing its economic structure?* I need a literature representing both sides of the argument:
- Yes, The economic structure must change. 
- No, the economic structure does not have to change.

AI proposed those two sides to be Structuralists and Neoclassical/Alternative Path theorists.

**Structuralists** propose a "natural path" from low-productivity sectors to high-productivity, high-capital sectors (e.g., agriculture $\rightarrow$ manufacturing $\rightarrow$ services). Key frameworks include the Lewis Dual-Sector Model, Kuznets’ stylized facts, Kaldor’s Growth Laws (manufacturing as the main engine of scale and spillovers), and Rodrik’s warning on premature deindustrialization—arguing that skipping industrial capability caps long-term growth.

**Neoclassical/Alternative Path** economists argue that countries do not have to follow 19th- and 20th-century industrial blueprints, and that the efficient allocation of resources, capital accumulation, and total factor productivity (Solow-Swan) matter more than sector shares. This camp points to modern exceptions: high-income resource exporters (e.g., Norway, Australia, GCC states), high-tech agro-economies, and nations leapfrogging directly into tradable, high-productivity services (e.g., IT and finance in India, Ireland, or Singapore).

**Takeaway for my thesis**: In my clustering analysis, Structuralism represents my primary hypothesis (sustained development requires transitioning across structural clusters), while Neoclassical/Alternative Path theories form the competing hypothesis (countries can reach high GDP/HDI while remaining within a resource- or service-dominant cluster).
